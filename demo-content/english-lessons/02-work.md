# Lesson 2 – Work and Careers

This lesson is about jobs, careers and the workplace. It has four parts: vocabulary, grammar (modal verbs for
obligation and advice), common mistakes, and useful phrases.

## 1. Vocabulary: work and careers

### 1.1 Jobs and contracts

- A **job** is the paid work you do (countable: *She has a new job*); **work** is usually uncountable (*I have a lot
  of work*).
- A **full-time** job is usually about 40 hours a week; a **part-time** job is fewer hours.
- A **permanent** contract has no end date; a **temporary** or **fixed-term** contract ends on a set date.
- **Salary** is the fixed amount paid per year or month; **wages** are usually paid per hour or per week.

### 1.2 Applying for a job

- You **apply for** a job by sending a **CV** (British English) or **résumé** (American English) and a **cover
  letter**.
- If the company likes your application, you are **shortlisted** and invited to an **interview**.
- The **employer** makes an **offer**; you **accept** or **turn down** the offer.

### 1.3 Leaving a job

- You **resign** or **quit** when you decide to leave; you **hand in your notice** to tell the employer.
- You are **fired** or **dismissed** when the employer ends your job because of something you did.
- You are **made redundant** (or **laid off**) when the company no longer needs your position.
- You **retire** when you stop working because of your age.

## 2. Grammar: modal verbs for obligation and advice

### 2.1 Obligation: must and have to

- **Must** expresses an obligation the speaker feels or a written rule: *I must call my mother. Visitors must sign
  in at reception.*
- **Have to** expresses an obligation from outside, such as a rule or a boss: *I have to wear a uniform at work.*
- For the past, use **had to**: *I had to work late yesterday.* (there is no past form of *must*)

### 2.2 No obligation and prohibition

- **Don't have to** means it is **not necessary**: *You don't have to come to the meeting; it's optional.*
- **Mustn't** means it is **not allowed**: *You mustn't share your password with anyone.*
- These two have very different meanings, although they look similar.

### 2.3 Advice: should and ought to

- **Should** and **ought to** give advice: *You should update your CV before you apply.*
- **Shouldn't** gives negative advice: *You shouldn't be late for an interview.*
- **Had better** gives strong advice, often with a warning: *You'd better leave now, or you'll miss the train.*

## 3. Common mistakes: work

### 3.1 Words that are often confused

- ✗ *I have a lot of works today.* → ✓ *I have a lot of work today.* (**work** is uncountable)
- ✗ *She found a new work.* → ✓ *She found a new job.*
- ✗ *My work colleagues are very sympathetic.* (when you mean nice) → ✓ *…very friendly / nice.* (**sympathetic**
  means showing understanding when someone has a problem)

### 3.2 Grammar mistakes

- ✗ *I must to finish this report.* → ✓ *I must finish this report.* (no **to** after modal verbs)
- ✗ *You mustn't come tomorrow, it's a holiday.* (when you mean it's not necessary) → ✓ *You don't have to come
  tomorrow.*
- ✗ *Yesterday I must work late.* → ✓ *Yesterday I had to work late.*

### 3.3 Prepositions

- ✗ *I work in Microsoft.* → ✓ *I work for Microsoft.* (**work for** a company, **work in** a department or a city)
- ✗ *I'm responsible of the budget.* → ✓ *I'm responsible for the budget.*

## 4. Useful phrases: work

### 4.1 In a job interview

- *I'd like to tell you a bit about my experience.*
- *One of my strengths is that I'm well organised.*
- *Could you tell me more about the team I would be working with?*

### 4.2 In meetings

- *Could we go back to the first point on the agenda?*
- *Sorry to interrupt, but could I add something?*
- *Let's sum up what we've agreed.*

### 4.3 Emails at work

- *I'm writing to ask about…*
- *Please find attached the report you asked for.*
- *I look forward to hearing from you.*
