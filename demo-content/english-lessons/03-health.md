# Lesson 3 – Health and Lifestyle

This lesson is about health, illness and a healthy lifestyle. It has four parts: vocabulary, grammar (first and
second conditional), common mistakes, and useful phrases.

## 1. Vocabulary: health and lifestyle

### 1.1 Feeling ill

- You **have a cold** (a mild illness with a runny nose and a sore throat) or **the flu** (a more serious illness
  with fever and aching muscles).
- A **headache**, **toothache** and **stomach ache** are pains in the head, tooth and stomach.
- You **have a temperature** or **a fever** when your body is hotter than normal.
- You **sprain** your ankle when you twist it badly; you **break** a bone.

### 1.2 Seeing a doctor

- You **make an appointment** with your **GP** (general practitioner, British English) or family doctor.
- The doctor **examines** you and may **prescribe** medicine; you take the **prescription** to a **pharmacy** (or
  **chemist's** in British English).
- A **check-up** is a routine examination when you are not ill.

### 1.3 A healthy lifestyle

- A **balanced diet** contains the right amounts of different kinds of food.
- To **work out** means to do physical exercise, often at a gym.
- To **cut down on** something means to eat, drink or do less of it: *cut down on sugar*.
- To **give up** something means to stop doing it completely: *give up smoking*.

## 2. Grammar: first and second conditional

### 2.1 First conditional

- Form: **if + present simple, will + infinitive**: *If you exercise regularly, you will feel better.*
- Use it for **real or likely** situations in the future: *If it rains tomorrow, we will stay at home.*
- **Unless** means *if … not*: *You won't get better unless you rest.*

### 2.2 Second conditional

- Form: **if + past simple, would + infinitive**: *If I had more time, I would go to the gym every day.*
- Use it for **unreal or unlikely** situations in the present or future: *If I won the lottery, I would travel
  around the world.*
- With the verb *be*, **were** is used for all persons in formal English: *If I were you, I would see a doctor.*

### 2.3 Choosing between them

- *If I get the job, I will move to London.* (it is possible – first conditional)
- *If I got the job, I would move to London.* (I don't think it will happen – second conditional)
- **If I were you…** is a common way to give advice.

## 3. Common mistakes: health

### 3.1 Words that are often confused

- ✗ *I have a pain in my stomach since yesterday.* → ✓ *I have had stomach ache since yesterday.*
- ✗ *The doctor gave me a recipe.* → ✓ *The doctor gave me a prescription.* (a **recipe** tells you how to cook
  something)
- ✗ *I'm sick of the flu.* → ✓ *I'm ill with the flu.* (**sick of** means tired of or annoyed by something)

### 3.2 Grammar mistakes

- ✗ *If I will have time, I will cook.* → ✓ *If I have time, I will cook.* (no **will** after *if*)
- ✗ *If I would have more money, I would buy a bike.* → ✓ *If I had more money, I would buy a bike.*
- ✗ *If I was you, I would rest.* → ✓ *If I were you, I would rest.* (in formal English)

### 3.3 Expressions

- ✗ *I do sport three times a week* is fine, but ✗ *I make sport* is wrong.
- ✗ *I went to the doctor's for a control.* → ✓ *I went to the doctor's for a check-up.*

## 4. Useful phrases: health

### 4.1 At the doctor's

- *I'd like to make an appointment, please.*
- *I've had a sore throat for three days.*
- *How often should I take these tablets?*

### 4.2 Talking about how you feel

- *I'm feeling a bit under the weather.* (slightly ill)
- *I'm much better now, thanks.*
- *I think I'm coming down with something.* (starting to become ill)

### 4.3 Giving health advice

- *You should drink plenty of water.*
- *Why don't you take a day off?*
- *If I were you, I'd get some rest.*
