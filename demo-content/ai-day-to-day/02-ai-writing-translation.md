# AI for Writing and Translation

AI writing tools draft, shorten, correct and translate text. This guide covers four subjects: how they work, good
uses, risks and limitations, and privacy and data security.

## 1. How AI writing and translation tools work

### 1.1 Language models

- AI writing tools use **large language models (LLMs)** that generate text one **token** (a word or part of a word)
  at a time.
- The model writes what is **statistically likely** to follow your instructions; it does not check facts unless it
  is connected to a search or to your documents.

### 1.2 Machine translation

- **Neural machine translation** translates whole sentences at once, using the surrounding words for context.
- Modern LLMs can also translate and adapt the **tone**, for example from formal to friendly.
- Translation quality is usually best for widely used languages and weaker for rare languages and specialist
  vocabulary.

### 1.3 Prompts

- The instruction you give is the **prompt**. Good prompts state the **audience**, the **goal**, the **length** and
  the **tone**.
- Giving an **example** of the style you want often works better than describing it.

## 2. Good uses of AI for writing and translation

### 2.1 Drafting

- A **first draft** of an email, report, job advert or meeting summary that you then edit.
- **Several versions** of a sentence or title to choose from.

### 2.2 Editing

- **Shortening** a long text, for example to a three-sentence summary.
- Correcting **grammar and spelling**, and making text easier to read.
- Changing the **tone**: more formal for a customer, simpler for a wide audience.

### 2.3 Translating

- Translating emails and documents to understand their content quickly.
- Translating your own text into another language as a **draft** that a fluent speaker checks.
- Keeping a **glossary** of company terms in the prompt, so that product names are translated consistently.

## 3. Risks and limitations of AI writing and translation

### 3.1 Invented facts

- AI can **hallucinate**: write confident statements, quotes, numbers or sources that are not true.
- Check every **fact, figure and reference** before sending or publishing a text.

### 3.2 Translation errors

- Translations can miss **idioms**, change the **meaning** of negations or mix up formal and informal address.
- **Legal, medical and contractual** texts must be checked by a qualified human translator.

### 3.3 Style and responsibility

- AI text can sound **generic**; readers may notice and trust it less.
- The person who sends a text is **responsible** for its content, whoever or whatever drafted it.
- Some publishers, schools and clients require you to **disclose** AI assistance.

## 4. Privacy and data security with AI writing tools

### 4.1 Confidential text

- Text pasted into an online AI tool is **sent to the provider**. Do not paste **confidential** documents, contracts
  or internal strategy into tools your organisation has not approved.
- Check whether the provider **stores** your text or uses it to **train** its models.

### 4.2 Personal data

- Emails and documents often contain **personal data**: names, addresses, health or salary information.
- Under the **GDPR**, personal data may only be shared with a service provider that has a proper agreement with
  your organisation. Remove or replace personal data before using a public tool.

### 4.3 Approved tools

- Use the **company-approved** AI writing tool with your work account.
- When unsure whether a text may be shared, ask your manager or the data protection officer first.
