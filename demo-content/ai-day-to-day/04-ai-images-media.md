# AI for Images, Audio and Video

Generative AI creates images, voices, music and video from a text description. This guide covers four subjects: how
it works, good uses, risks and limitations, and privacy and data security.

## 1. How AI image and media generation works

### 1.1 Diffusion models

- Most image generators use **diffusion models**. Training teaches the model to remove **noise** from images step by
  step.
- To create a new image, the model starts from **random noise** and removes noise in many steps, guided by the text
  description, the **prompt**.

### 1.2 Prompts for images

- A good image prompt describes the **subject**, the **style** (photo, illustration, watercolour), the
  **composition** and the **lighting**.
- A **negative prompt** lists what should not appear in the image.
- **Image editing** tools change part of an existing picture, for example replacing the background.

### 1.3 Audio and video

- **Text-to-speech** turns text into natural-sounding speech in many voices and languages.
- **Voice cloning** imitates a specific person's voice from a short recording.
- **Text-to-video** models generate short video clips from a prompt or a still image.

## 2. Good uses of AI images and media

### 2.1 Presentations and documents

- **Illustrations** and icons for presentations, when no suitable stock image exists.
- **Mock-ups** of a product, screen or poster to discuss an idea before a designer works on it.

### 2.2 Marketing and social media

- **Variations** of a banner in several formats and colour schemes.
- **Background removal** and simple photo retouching.

### 2.3 Accessibility and learning

- **Voice-overs** for training videos in several languages.
- **Subtitles** and transcripts generated from speech, which help people who are deaf or hard of hearing.

## 3. Risks and limitations of AI images and media

### 3.1 Errors in generated images

- Generated images can contain **mistakes**: extra fingers, unreadable text on signs, impossible objects.
- Check every image before using it, especially **text and logos** in the picture.

### 3.2 Deepfakes and misinformation

- **Deepfakes** are realistic fake images, voices or videos of real people.
- Criminals use **cloned voices** in phone scams, for example pretending to be a manager who urgently asks for a
  payment. Always confirm unusual requests through a **second channel**.
- Many tools add **content credentials** (the **C2PA** standard) or invisible **watermarks** to show that media was
  made with AI.

### 3.3 Copyright

- Whether AI-generated images are **protected by copyright** differs between countries.
- Images can resemble **existing works or brands**; do not use generated images that imitate a known artist, logo
  or character.
- Follow the image tool's **licence terms** for commercial use.

## 4. Privacy and data security with AI images and media

### 4.1 Photos and voices of real people

- A photo or voice recording of a person is **personal data**. Do not upload colleagues' or customers' photos or
  recordings to AI tools without their **consent**.
- Never create images or audio of a **real person** that could embarrass or mislead.

### 4.2 Labelling AI media

- The **EU AI Act** requires that deepfakes, meaning AI-generated or manipulated images, audio or video of real
  people, places or events, are **clearly labelled** as artificially generated.
- Label AI-generated images in internal and external communication, for example "Image generated with AI".

### 4.3 Uploaded material

- Files uploaded for editing are **sent to the provider**. Do not upload confidential designs, unreleased products
  or internal documents to tools your organisation has not approved.
