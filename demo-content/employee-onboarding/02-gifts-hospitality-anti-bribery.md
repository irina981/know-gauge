# Gifts, Hospitality and Anti-Bribery Policy

## 1. Purpose and principle

Corvane has **zero tolerance for bribery and corruption**. A bribe is anything of value offered, promised, given,
requested or accepted to improperly influence a business decision. "Anything of value" includes cash, gifts, meals,
travel, job offers for relatives, donations, discounts and favours. The size of the bribe does not matter: a €20
payment is still a bribe.

Reasonable and proportionate gifts and hospitality are a normal part of building business relationships. This policy
explains where the line is.

## 2. Gifts: value thresholds

All values are the estimated market value per person, including VAT.

| Value of a single gift | What you must do |
|---|---|
| Up to €50 | You may give or accept it. No registration needed. |
| More than €50 and up to €150 | Get approval from your manager **before** giving it, or within 5 working days of receiving it, and record it in the Gifts & Hospitality Register. |
| More than €150 | Decline or return it. Only the Ethics & Compliance Office can grant an exception, in writing. |

The **annual limit** is €200 in total from, or to, the same company or person in a calendar year. Gifts that are
individually below €50 still count towards the annual limit.

If returning a gift would cause serious offence (for example a ceremonial gift during a visit abroad), you may accept
it on behalf of Corvane, register it and hand it to the Ethics & Compliance Office, which will display it in the
office or donate it to charity.

## 3. Gifts that are never allowed

Regardless of value, you must never give or accept:

- cash or cash equivalents, including gift cards, vouchers, cryptocurrency and pre-paid cards;
- gifts during an active tender or contract negotiation with that company or person (the **tender quiet period**
  starts when the request for proposal is issued and ends when the contract is signed or the tender is cancelled);
- gifts that you requested or asked someone else to request;
- gifts given in secret, sent to a private address to avoid detection, or given to a family member instead of you;
- anything that is illegal or would embarrass Corvane if made public.

## 4. Hospitality

Hospitality means meals, drinks, events, entertainment, travel and accommodation.

- Business meals up to **€100 per person** per event are allowed without pre-approval, as long as there is a clear
  business purpose and a Corvane employee attends.
- Hospitality above €100 per person needs pre-approval from the Ethics & Compliance Office.
- Event tickets (sports, concerts) are only acceptable if the host attends. Tickets without the host are treated as
  a gift and follow the gift thresholds.
- Corvane never pays for travel or accommodation of customers or their family members, except for documented
  product training or site visits approved in advance by the Ethics & Compliance Office. Spouses and partners are
  never included.
- The same business contact should not be invited more than **four times** in a calendar year.

## 5. Public officials

A public official is anyone who works for a government, a government agency, a state-owned company, a public
university or hospital, or an international organisation, and also political party officials and candidates.

Any gift or hospitality, **of any value**, to a public official requires prior written approval from the Ethics &
Compliance Office. A branded pen or notebook from a conference stand is the only exception.

## 6. Facilitation payments

Facilitation payments are small unofficial payments to speed up routine government actions, such as issuing a visa
or clearing goods through customs. They are **prohibited** at Corvane, even where they are common local practice.

The only exception is a payment made under **duress**, when you face an immediate threat to your health, safety or
liberty. Your safety comes first: make the payment, get to safety, and report it to the Ethics & Compliance Office
within **24 hours**. You will not be disciplined for a payment made under duress.

## 7. Third parties

Corvane can be liable for bribes paid by resellers, agents, consultants and other third parties acting on its behalf.
Before engaging any third party that will interact with customers or public officials:

1. complete the third-party due diligence questionnaire in the Compliance Portal;
2. make sure the contract includes Corvane's anti-bribery clause;
3. watch for red flags, such as requests for payment in cash, payment to a bank account in a different country from
   where the service is provided, unusually high commissions (above 15% of contract value) or a refusal to sign the
   anti-bribery clause.

## 8. Donations and sponsorships

Charitable donations and sponsorships must be approved by the Corporate Social Responsibility Committee. Political
donations on behalf of Corvane are not allowed in any country.

## 9. The Gifts & Hospitality Register

The Register is part of the Compliance Portal. Each entry must include the date, the giver and receiver, the
companies involved, a description, the estimated value and the business purpose. The Ethics & Compliance Office
reviews the Register every quarter and reports patterns, such as repeated gifts from one supplier, to the Audit
Committee.

## 10. Examples

- A supplier sends you a bottle of wine worth about €30 at the end of the year. **Allowed**: no registration needed,
  as long as it is not during a tender with that supplier.
- A customer offers you a €100 gift card after a successful go-live. **Not allowed**: gift cards are cash
  equivalents. Decline politely.
- A potential supplier invites you to a €250-per-person dinner while you are evaluating their bid. **Not allowed**:
  this is within the tender quiet period and above the hospitality limit.
- A customs officer asks for €40 "to process faster". **Not allowed**: this is a facilitation payment. Refuse,
  ask for an official receipt for any official fee, and report it.

## 11. Reporting

Report any suspected bribery, or any request for a bribe, to the Ethics & Compliance Office or through the Corvane
Ethics Line as soon as possible. Breaches of this policy can lead to dismissal and may be reported to the
authorities.
