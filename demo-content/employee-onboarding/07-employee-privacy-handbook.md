# Employee Privacy Handbook (GDPR Basics)

## 1. Why privacy matters at Corvane

Corvane builds logistics-planning software used by customers across Europe. Our products process personal data
about our customers' employees, drivers and end customers. We also process personal data about our own employees and
candidates. The EU **General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR)** sets the rules. Breaking them can lead to fines of
up to **€20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover**, whichever is higher, and, just as importantly, to a loss of
customer trust.

Questions about privacy go to the Data Protection Officer (DPO) at **privacy@corvane.example**.

## 2. Key terms

- **Personal data**: any information relating to an identified or identifiable living person. Names, email addresses,
  phone numbers, IP addresses, GPS locations of a vehicle linked to a driver, employee IDs and photos are all personal
  data.
- **Special category data**: personal data that needs extra protection: health, racial or ethnic origin, political
  opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, genetic and biometric data, and sex life or sexual orientation.
  At Corvane, special category data is always classified as **Restricted**.
- **Processing**: anything done with personal data, including collecting, storing, viewing, sharing and deleting it.
- **Controller**: the organisation that decides why and how personal data is processed. Corvane is the controller for
  employee data and for its own marketing data.
- **Processor**: an organisation that processes personal data on behalf of a controller. Corvane is a processor for
  the data customers put into Corvane products.
- **Data subject**: the person the data is about.

## 3. The seven GDPR principles

1. **Lawfulness, fairness and transparency**: have a legal basis and tell people what you do with their data.
2. **Purpose limitation**: collect data for a specified purpose and do not reuse it for an incompatible one.
3. **Data minimisation**: collect only the data you actually need.
4. **Accuracy**: keep data correct and up to date.
5. **Storage limitation**: keep data only as long as necessary (see the Retention Schedule).
6. **Integrity and confidentiality**: protect data with appropriate security.
7. **Accountability**: be able to show that you follow the other principles.

## 4. Legal bases

Every processing activity needs one of the six legal bases in the GDPR:

| Legal basis | Typical Corvane example |
|---|---|
| Consent | Sending the product newsletter to people who signed up. |
| Contract | Processing an employee's bank details to pay their salary. |
| Legal obligation | Sharing salary data with the tax authority. |
| Vital interests | Sharing health information with paramedics in an emergency. |
| Public task | Rarely relevant for Corvane. |
| Legitimate interests | Logging access to Corvane systems for security purposes. |

Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, and it must be as easy to withdraw as to give.
Because of the imbalance of power, Corvane does **not** rely on consent for most employee data.

## 5. Rights of data subjects

People have the right to:

- **access** their data (a "data subject access request", or DSAR);
- have inaccurate data **rectified**;
- have data **erased** in certain cases (the "right to be forgotten");
- **restrict** processing;
- **data portability** (receive their data in a machine-readable format);
- **object** to processing based on legitimate interests, and to direct marketing at any time;
- not be subject to decisions based **solely on automated processing** that significantly affect them.

Corvane must respond to a request within **one month**. This can be extended by **two further months** for complex
requests, but the person must be told about the extension within the first month.

**What you must do:** if anyone asks about their personal data, in any words and through any channel, forward the
request to privacy@corvane.example within **2 working days**. Do not answer it yourself. If the request is about
data in a customer's Corvane product, the DPO will forward it to the customer, who is the controller.

## 6. Privacy by design and DPIAs

New products, features and internal tools must consider privacy from the start: collect less, pseudonymise where
possible, set short retention periods and restrict access by default.

A **Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)** must be completed and approved by the DPO **before** starting any
processing that is likely to be high risk, for example:

- large-scale processing of special category data;
- systematic monitoring of people, such as continuous vehicle tracking linked to drivers;
- new technologies such as AI models trained on personal data;
- combining data sets from different sources.

## 7. Sharing data and international transfers

- Personal data may only be shared with suppliers that have signed a **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)** with Corvane
  and appear on the approved sub-processor list.
- Transfers of personal data outside the European Economic Area (EEA) need an approved transfer mechanism, such as an
  adequacy decision or Standard Contractual Clauses. Ask the DPO before using any new tool hosted outside the EEA.

## 8. Employee data processed by Corvane

Corvane processes your personal data for HR administration, payroll, security, IT support and legal obligations, as
described in the Employee Privacy Notice on the intranet. You can see most of your data in PeoplePortal and request
corrections there. Access to HR data is limited to the People Team, Payroll and, for their own team members,
managers.

## 9. Everyday do's and don'ts

- Do use customer data only for the purpose it was provided for, and only in approved systems.
- Do use anonymised or synthetic data in test and demo environments. **Production personal data must never be copied
  into test environments** without DPO approval.
- Don't download customer data to your laptop "to analyse it later".
- Don't discuss individual employees' health or personal situations in open channels.
- Report any loss or exposure of personal data to the Security Desk within **1 hour** (see the Personal Data Breach
  Response Procedure).
